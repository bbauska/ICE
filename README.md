People seeking to stop or push back against U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) generally take political, local, legal, and community-based actions advocated by civil rights and immigrant advocacy groups.

1. Pressure Federal Lawmakers

• Demand oversight and funding limits: Organizations like the National Immigration Law Center urge residents to contact members of Congress to conduct oversight, oppose bills expanding ICE surveillance or enforcement authority, and curb deportation funding.
• Advocate to abolish or restrict ICE: Groups such as Amnesty International USA and the American Friends Service Committee call on Congress to abolish ICE, close detention centers, and end detention and deportations.

2. Push Local and State Officials

• Limit local cooperation: Advocacy groups recommend pressing city, county, and state officials—as well as local schools—to adopt sanctuary policies, stop sharing data with ICE, and end local law enforcement collaboration.
• Challenge state-level policies: Residents can urge state legislators to pass protections limiting federal enforcement tactics in local communities.

3. Support Communities and Legal Efforts

• Join rapid response and accompaniment: Advocacy groups suggest volunteering with local rapid response networks, helping immigrants access legal support, and accompanying individuals to court hearings or routine check-ins.
• Share "Know Your Rights" information: Civil rights groups like the League of Women Voters encourage sharing resources on constitutional rights during encounters with federal agents, including the right to remain silent and the requirement for a judicial warrant to enter non-public areas.
• Document and observe safely: Immigrant rights guides advise following local laws when filming or observing enforcement activity without interfering or obstructing officers. On Reddit's Seattle forum, opinions are mixed on informal community tactics—some users suggest walking coworkers to their cars or using workplace alert codes, while others argue that presence mainly creates a witness rather than preventing agents from entering non-public areas without a warrant.
(Note: If you are looking for official government reporting channels rather than advocacy, the ICE Tip Form is provided by the agency to submit tips to law enforcement.)
